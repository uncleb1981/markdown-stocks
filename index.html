FAQ

What is this?
A stock that just dropped isn't automatically a good value — most of the time it dropped for a reason. Down, Not Out looks for the smaller set that fell 10%+ in the past month but still hold up on the fundamentals: priced reasonably against their own growth rate (not just a low P/E), genuinely profitable, and not showing signs of financial distress. The goal is separating names that look like real value from names that are just falling. It's a personal research tool, not a stock-picking service.
How are the 7 picks chosen?
Every name has to pass all of these: down 10%+ in the last month · P/E and forward P/E under 22 · PEG under 1 (priced well relative to its growth rate — the classic "undervalued" threshold, not just a low P/E) · net profit margin over 3% and return on equity over 5% (rules out financially fragile companies) · RSI under 45 (actually oversold, not just recently down) · trading volume under its own 30-day average (hasn't been widely noticed yet) · market cap over $300M · no banks or pharmaceutical companies. Whatever clears those filters gets ranked by market cap — the top 7 make the cut, though some days fewer than 7 names pass everything.
Where does the explanation under each stock come from?
It's built from real reported earnings — beat or miss vs. estimates, and the report date — not a news search. A beat that still sold off is flagged as likely guidance-driven; a miss is called out directly. It won't catch a specific event (a downgrade, a lawsuit, a recall) unless that shows up in the earnings numbers themselves.
How long should I hold one of these?
There's no set answer, and this app won't pretend to give you a specific number of weeks or months. Instead, each card shows two objective checkpoints: the stock's next earnings report (the next scheduled moment new information could confirm or break the thesis), and the RSI level that would need to clear 45 for the stock to stop counting as oversold in the first place. Once a pick no longer meets the same criteria that got it here, the original setup is gone — that's a more honest signal than a fixed holding period.
Is this investment advice?
No. It's informational only, not a licensed recommendation, and it never places trades on its own.
